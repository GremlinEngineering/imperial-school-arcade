PRESERVE THE GAME'S ARTISTIC WORLD:
Render the game-specific artistic traits established in POSTER DESIGN.art_direction,
recognition and visual_prompt alongside its scene and regional release context.
Preserve the setting AND the illustration language: line and shape treatment,
shading, palette, costumes, equipment, architecture and mood. A Japanese title,
platform label, castle or sword alone does not preserve that artistic world.
Adapt the reference Gremlin and Fox to its rendering and costumes while retaining
their reference identity, anatomy and adult proportions. The character reference
must not pull every game into one house illustration style.
Do not replace an anime-influenced fantasy treatment with generic dark painterly
fantasy, or replace another game's distinctive world with universal cinematic
realism, steampunk or sepia. Painting and anime character design can coexist;
do not flatten a painted original merely because its characters are anime-influenced.
Follow the particular game and release, not a rule that a country equals a style.
Keep period print treatment subordinate to the artwork; ageing and heavy grain
are not required to make a vintage game recognizable.

COHERENT COMPOSITION AND EMBODIMENT:
Render the intended overall visual idea, with every included character integrated
into its action or graphic structure. Do not append Fox as an unrelated extra figure.
Respect the designed hierarchy, relative scale, depth, movement and relationships.
Within a physical scene, bodies must plausibly continue behind occluders; make
seating, support, cockpit rims and grips spatially readable. A vehicle in front of
a torso is not by itself a plausible seat. Avoid ambiguous fusion of figure and
machine, unexplained floating torsos and changes of scale within one physical plane.
Natural occlusion is allowed; do not expose every limb just to prove anatomy.
An intentional portrait montage is allowed: use clear graphic grouping, boundaries
or transitions so portraits cannot be mistaken for incomplete physical bodies.
Do not force all posters into literal action scenes or force both characters to
perform the same action. Integrate their distinct roles into one composition.

Use poster_text / expected_text as authoritative lettering. For arcade releases,
the platform lettering is "Arcade", not a Russian translation. Title contains
only the game name; release year and platform are separate metadata, not part
of the title. Draft layout descriptions cannot override the expected lettering.


LAYOUT FREEDOM (applies also when an earlier design brief specifies coordinates):
Title, platform and year placement is an editorial design choice, not a validation
constraint. A centered bottom line, corner block, side strip, or another readable
arrangement can be valid. Adapt the composition to the platform, region, period
and illustration. Do not enforce exact coordinates, alignment, text-block size,
or a particular corner. Preserve correct readable text without clipping, accidental
duplication, or overlaps that obscure it. An arrangement differing from the draft
layout alone is NOT a defect and must NOT cause fail or uncertain.

CHARACTER IDENTITY IS A HARD CONSTRAINT, above period styling and game-role
adaptation. Depict this specific reference Gremlin in a new scene. Preserve the
reference head silhouette, facial proportions and body build; do not redesign
him as a generic goblin, orc, imp, baby mascot or realistic movie creature.
Gremlin identity anchors visible in the supplied reference:
- compact, sturdy body and a broad head, keeping the reference head-to-body ratio;
- very large pointed ears extending sideways, with warm orange inner surfaces,
  green outer edges and the characteristic irregular lower contours;
- large amber-golden eyes with dark round pupils, shaped expressive brows and
  the reference eye spacing; no blank yellow eyes or permanently angry squint;
- a short broad green nose with defined nostrils, rounded cheek volumes and a
  wide expressive mouth; no long snout, tiny button nose or heavy protruding jaw;
- small irregular ivory pointed teeth when visible, following the reference,
  not two oversized orc tusks or a new tooth arrangement invented for the role;
- green skin with restrained fine texture and the reference tufts along the
  cheeks and temples; retain these identifying features under the chosen rendering technique.
Keep his alert, knowing character; expression may change naturally with the
scene without changing facial anatomy or forcing a permanent grin. Costume,
pose and lighting can vary. Keep the base colors recognizable under lighting.
Do not copy the anatomy or facial design of the game's original hero onto him.
Scale the character within the composition without changing his proportions;
when the Fox appears, preserve their reference relative height and her identity.

When include_fox=true, preserve this specific adult feminine anthropomorphic
Fox from the reference, with the same care as the Gremlin:
- taller, more slender adult proportions than the compact Gremlin; preserve her

  around the shoulders, kept recognizable around any scene-appropriate helmet;

- orange fur, pale throat/chest fur where visible, and one full bushy tail with

She remains the same adult character in movement and expressive poses. Do not
turn her into a child, a generic fox mascot or a male-coded game-hero redesign.

ANATOMY AND PRINT CLARITY:
Plan a coherent connected body and plausible stance before adding scenery.
Show attached limbs and footwear; use natural occlusion where necessary.
Never leave an isolated boot, a torso cut off by the floor, extra limbs,
or hands fused into props. Make object grips and contact with the floor clear.
Use a readable advertising silhouette and focal hierarchy. Preserve expressive
rendering and release-era graphic design with clean separations and legible text.
Do not apply blanket sepia, distressed-paper wear or heavy grain as a retro shortcut.
Warm colors and subtle print texture are allowed when appropriate to the game.
