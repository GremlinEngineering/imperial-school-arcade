# Journal and personal play experience

The journal records the progress of play: returning to a game, earning an achievement, finishing a game, making a discovery or competing for a leaderboard position. Achievement counts and the author's impressions are different accounts of the same experience.

## Events and states

“Playing” is a profile state. “Achievement earned” is an event and may coexist with a personal record or a newly observed game. Beaten, completed and mastered retain their source-specific meanings; they are not a mandatory progression for every game.

A rank drop compares leaderboard results or snapshots; it need not mean a worse personal attempt. A personal record is not necessarily a world record. A game newly appearing in the data does not establish the player's first-ever launch.

Authored recollection, series context and historical interpretation sit alongside reference information. Links, calendars and timelines offer different ways to read the journal. Music and artwork belong to the same game but do not prove an achievement.

Dynamic themes map explicitly supplied events to visual features: an achievement can bring a pearl, while a rank drop can turn over a sign. An unavailable profile must not become a fictional achievement. See the [dynamic wallpaper specification](dynamic-wallpapers.md) for the codes and rules.

## Personal results and historical recognition

Achievements, records and completion describe the player. Recognition describes the game through historical lists, rankings and confirmed sources. They are separate kinds of evidence, not one award scale. Authored impressions and editorial interpretation retain their roles alongside facts.
