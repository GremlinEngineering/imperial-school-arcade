# Views and wallpaper collections

A collection groups related pictures and declared device formats. Clients select formats from metadata; a folder name is not a universal display-detection rule.

| Collection | Selection |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary, monthly, seasonal | The available set and calendar policy |
| Weather | Artwork, weather source and forecast interval |
| Dynamic | The current ready-made frame for a device |

Monthly and seasonal sets may offer twelve and four calendar states. Weather collections add day periods, weather groups and supported Moon phases. Dynamic themes use conditional layers.


## Two approaches to screens

Screen shape affects the story. Characters can interact across a wide monitor; the same arrangement on a portrait display can make them tiny or put important action outside the frame. Collections therefore use two approaches.

| Approach | Preserved | Adapted | Suitable when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A screen-specific story | World, visual language and meaning of states | Camera, setting, character actions and details | The proportions support another scene or the old staging loses its meaning |
| Recomposition of one scene | Story, main action and participants | Placement, scale, surroundings and open space | The same action remains readable in different proportions |

A separate story makes formats different windows into one world. Recomposition keeps the same scene recognisable across devices. Both belong in the project; a new pixel size alone does not require a new story.

For example, desktop Teahouse shows a room while the phone foundation shows a tiered pavilion and inhabited terrace. They share a world and inhabitants but have different staging and weather cues. A collection retaining the same action can rearrange it for portrait or wide displays. Simple cropping is appropriate only when all meaningful parts survive.

Artwork is reviewed at actual viewing sizes, including faces and events in reduction, clocks, notifications, icon grids and expected crops. These concerns belong to wallpaper composition.

Desktop, ultrawide and phone pictures need distinct compositions. Each dynamic device owns its artwork, dimensions and rules; it must not borrow another device's layers or stretch a desktop scene into a phone design. Clients read declared identifiers, including historical differences between collection families. `desktop_3456x2234` names one dynamic composition, not every Mac.

A ZIP belongs to a collection and format, not the whole project. Previews help selection, originals serve wallpaper installation, and JSON describes their state mapping. Applications may offer viewers and slideshows. Content changes at stable URLs are distinguished through published versions and SHA-256.
